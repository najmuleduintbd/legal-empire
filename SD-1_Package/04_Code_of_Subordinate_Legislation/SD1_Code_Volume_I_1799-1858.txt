PREFACE
================================================================================

This Volume I of the Code of Delegated/Subordinate Legislation of Bangladesh
(Package SD-1) covers the foundational period of 1799-1858, during which the
Bengal Presidency was governed by the East India Company under successive
Charter Acts granted by the British Parliament.

The subordinate legislations of this period — primarily the Bengal Regulations
(1793-1834) and rules/orders made under post-1834 Acts — represent the
earliest layer of codified law applicable to the territory of present-day
Bangladesh. While most of these instruments have been repealed or superseded,
they remain historically significant as the foundation upon which the entire
subsequent legal framework was built.

This Volume is organized thematically by subject matter, with each entry
providing the full citation, parent authority, scope, current status, and
relevant cross-references.

CHAPTER 1: LAND AND REVENUE
================================================================================

This chapter contains all subordinate legislations relating to land tenure,
revenue administration, survey and settlement, rent assessment and recovery,
and related matters during 1799-1858.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Land and revenue formed the backbone of governance in Bengal under the East
India Company. The Permanent Settlement of 1793 (implemented through Bengal
Regulation VIII of 1793) created the zamindari system that shaped land
relations in Bengal until its abolition by the East Bengal State Acquisition
and Tenancy Act, 1950. The subordinate legislations in this chapter
established the administrative framework for revenue collection, tenant
protection, survey operations, and dispute resolution.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1   BENGAL REGULATION VIII OF 1793
      Regulation for the Permanent Settlement of the Revenues of
      Bengal, Bihar and Orissa

      Date of Enactment    : 22 March 1793
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council (Lord Cornwallis)
      Legal Basis          : Charter Act of 1793
      Gazette Reference    : Bengal Gazette, 1793
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa Provinces
      Commencement         : 22 March 1793

      PROVISIONS:
      - Establishment of the Permanent Settlement — fixed revenue
        assessment on zamindari estates in perpetuity
      - Definition of zamindari rights and obligations
      - Revenue payment schedules and procedures
      - Penalties for default in revenue payment
      - Procedure for sale of defaulting estates (sunset law)
      - Protection of tenant rights under zamindars
      - Board of Revenue oversight powers

      STATUS: Historical / Superseded
      - The zamindari system was abolished by the East Bengal State
        Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950 (Act XXVIII of 1951)
      - However, the principles of the Permanent Settlement influenced
        all subsequent land legislation in Bengal

      CROSS-REFERENCES:
      - Bengal Regulation I of 1799 (Revenue Administration)
      - Bengal Regulation I of 1800 (Decennial Settlement)
      - Bengal Regulation V of 1812 (Revenue Arrears Sale)
      - Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885
      - East Bengal State Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950

      HISTORICAL NOTES:
      The Permanent Settlement is one of the most consequential pieces of
      legislation in South Asian history. By fixing revenue demands and
      creating a class of zamindars as intermediary landlords, it shaped
      social, economic, and political life in Bengal for over 150 years.
      Its ultimate replacement by the SAT Act of 1950 was a landmark of
      post-independence land reform.

1.7   BENGAL REGULATION XI OF 1825
      Bengal Alluvion and Diluvion Regulation

      Date of Enactment    : 1825
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal Presidency

      PROVISIONS:
      - Rights regarding newly formed alluvial land (chars)
      - Impact of diluvion (river erosion) on land rights
      - Re-formation rights when eroded land resurfaces
      - Revenue liability for alluvial and diluvial land
      - Survey procedure for changed land formations

      STATUS: Partially in Force
      - Core principles regarding alluvion and diluvion rights have been
        incorporated into subsequent legislation and remain relevant
      - Particularly important for riverine areas of Bangladesh

      CROSS-REFERENCES:
      - State Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950
      - Survey and Settlement Rules
      - Relevant Supreme Court of Bangladesh decisions

      HISTORICAL NOTES:
      Given Bangladesh's extensive river system and the constant formation
      and erosion of char lands, the principles established by this
      regulation remain directly relevant to contemporary land disputes.

CHAPTER 2: CIVIL JUSTICE AND PROCEDURE
================================================================================

2.1   BENGAL REGULATION III OF 1793
      Regulation for the Administration of Civil Justice

      Date of Enactment    : 1793
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council (Lord Cornwallis)
      Legal Basis          : Charter Act of 1793
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa

      PROVISIONS:
      - Establishment of civil courts at district level (Diwani Adalat)
      - Provincial courts of appeal (Sadar Diwani Adalat)
      - Jurisdiction of civil courts
      - Civil procedure (pleadings, evidence, decree execution)
      - Court fees
      - Role of native law officers (pandits and maulvis)

      STATUS: Repealed / Superseded
      SUCCESSOR: Code of Civil Procedure, 1859/1882/1908

      HISTORICAL NOTES:
      This regulation established the three-tier civil court system in
      Bengal: District Courts → Provincial Courts of Appeal → Sadar Diwani
      Adalat. This structure evolved into the modern court hierarchy.

CHAPTER 3: CRIMINAL JUSTICE AND PROCEDURE
================================================================================

3.1   BENGAL REGULATION IV OF 1793
      Regulation for the Administration of Criminal Justice

      Date of Enactment    : 1793
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council
      Legal Basis          : Charter Act of 1793
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa

      PROVISIONS:
      - Establishment of criminal courts (Nizamat Adalat)
      - Magisterial jurisdiction
      - Criminal procedure
      - Punishments (based partly on Muhammadan criminal law initially)
      - Role of Qazis and Muftis in criminal proceedings

      STATUS: Repealed / Superseded
      SUCCESSOR: Indian Penal Code, 1860; Code of Criminal Procedure, 1861/1898

      HISTORICAL NOTES:
      This regulation initially retained elements of Islamic criminal law
      (Muhammadan criminal law) for punishments, but these were gradually
      replaced by secular penal provisions, culminating in the Indian
      Penal Code of 1860.

      Bengal State Prisoners Regulation

      Date of Enactment    : 1818
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal Presidency

      - Grounds for detention: state security, public order
      - Conditions of detention
      - Review procedure

      continued in subsequent legislation.

CHAPTER 5: PERSONAL LAW, FAMILY, AND SUCCESSION
================================================================================

5.1   BENGAL REGULATION IV OF 1793 (Section on Personal Law)
      Application of Hindu and Muhammadan Personal Law

      Date of Enactment    : 1793
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal Presidency

      PROVISIONS:
      - Application of Hindu personal law to Hindus in matters of
        succession, inheritance, marriage, caste, and religious usage
      - Application of Muhammadan (Islamic) personal law to Muslims in
        matters of succession, inheritance, marriage, dower, and
        religious observance
      - Role of pandits and maulvis as law officers in personal law cases

      STATUS: Partially Superseded
      - The principle of applying personal law to respective communities
        continues in Bangladesh
      - Specific provisions superseded by codified personal laws

      CROSS-REFERENCES:
      - Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937
      - Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961
      - Succession Act, 1925

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.2   BENGAL REGULATION VII OF 1829 (Regulation XVII of 1829)
      Abolition of Sati (Suttee)

      Date of Enactment    : 4 December 1829
      Authority            : Governor-General in Council (Lord William Bentinck)
      Territorial Scope    : Bengal Presidency (later extended to all India)

      PROVISIONS:
      - Prohibition of the practice of sati (widow immolation)
      - Criminal penalties for abetting or encouraging sati
      - Enforcement mechanisms

      STATUS: Historical — Absorbed into criminal law
      SUCCESSOR: Indian Penal Code, 1860 (provisions on culpable homicide
                 and abetment)

      HISTORICAL NOTES:
      This regulation was a landmark of social reform in British India,
      abolishing the practice of sati in Bengal. It was enacted after
      sustained advocacy by reformer Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
