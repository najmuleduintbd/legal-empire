1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND
================================================================================

The Government of the People's Republic of Bangladesh, through the Legislative
and Parliamentary Affairs Division under the Ministry of Law, Justice and
Parliamentary Affairs, has initiated the "Strengthening Legislative Capacity
and Legal Awareness Building Project" (Project Code: 224359600). A key
component of this project is the preparation of a comprehensive Code of
Delegated/Subordinate Legislation of Bangladesh.

Package SD-1 specifically covers the identification, collection, analysis,
classification, and codification of ALL delegated/subordinate legislations
(rules, regulations, by-laws, orders, notifications, circulars, etc.) enacted,
promulgated, or issued during the period 1799 to 1970.

This Inception Report outlines our understanding of the assignment, the
methodology to be adopted, sources to be consulted, the classification
framework, work plan, team deployment, and quality assurance measures.

================================================================================
2. SCOPE OF PACKAGE SD-1
================================================================================

Package SD-1 encompasses the following:

   a) Systematic identification of ALL delegated/subordinate legislations
      issued during 1799-1970 that are applicable or were applicable to the
      territory of present-day Bangladesh.

   b) Collection and compilation of the full texts of identified subordinate
      legislations from official gazettes, government archives, and other
      authoritative sources.

   c) Legal analysis of each subordinate legislation, including:
      - Identification of the parent/enabling Act
      - Classification by subject matter, ministry, and legal authority
      - Determination of current status (active, amended, repealed, obsolete)
      - Cross-referencing with subsequent amendments and repeals

   d) Preparation of a structured Code of Delegated/Subordinate Legislation
      with appropriate indexing, cross-referencing, and annotation.

3. HISTORICAL CONTEXT (1799-1970)
================================================================================

The period 1799-1970 spans three distinct political and legal eras, each with
its own legislative framework and system of delegated legislation:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ERA 1: EAST INDIA COMPANY AND EARLY BRITISH RULE (1799-1858)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Period     : 1799-1858
Territory  : Bengal Presidency (including present-day Bangladesh)
Sovereign  : East India Company / British Crown (after 1858)

Key Legislative Milestones:
   - Bengal Regulations (1799-1834): The earliest codified regulations for
     Bengal, enacted by the Governor-General in Council. These regulations
     governed revenue collection, judicial administration, police, land
     tenure, and various civil and criminal matters.

   - Charter Act of 1833: Centralized legislative power in the Governor-
     General of India in Council. Bengal Regulations ceased after 1834,
     replaced by Acts of the central legislature.

   - Charter Act of 1853: Further legislative reforms.

Types of Subordinate Legislation:
   - Bengal Regulations (quasi-legislative in nature)
   - Governor-General's Orders and Notifications
   - Revenue Board Circulars and Orders
   - Judicial Circulars
   - Executive Orders under Regulations

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ERA 2: BRITISH CROWN RULE (1858-1947)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Period     : 1858-1947
Territory  : Bengal Province / East Bengal (present-day Bangladesh)
Sovereign  : British Crown

Key Legislative Milestones:
   - Government of India Act 1858: Transfer of power from EIC to Crown
   - Indian Councils Act 1861: Provincial legislative councils established
   - Indian Councils Act 1892: Expanded legislative councils
   - Government of India Act 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms)
   - Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms): Dyarchy
     introduced; provincial governments gained rule-making powers
   - Government of India Act 1935: Provincial autonomy; expanded delegated
     legislation powers
   - Partition of Bengal 1905 (reversed 1911): Created Eastern Bengal and
     Assam province
   - Partition of India 1947: East Bengal became part of Pakistan

Types of Subordinate Legislation:
   - Rules framed under Acts of the Central Legislature
   - Rules framed under Acts of the Bengal Legislative Council/Assembly
   - Regulations under various Codes (CPC, CrPC, IPC)
   - Municipal by-laws and local body regulations
   - Notifications in the official Gazette
   - Executive Orders and Government Resolutions
   - Revenue and land tenure rules
   - Educational regulations
   - Police and prison rules

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ERA 3: PAKISTAN PERIOD (1947-1970)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Period     : 1947-1970
Territory  : East Pakistan (East Bengal) — present-day Bangladesh
Sovereign  : Dominion/Republic of Pakistan

Key Legislative Milestones:
   - Independence Act 1947: Pakistan created; inherited British Indian laws
   - Government of India (Adaptation) Order 1947
   - Constitution of Pakistan 1956 (first constitution)
   - Martial Law 1958-1962 (Ayub Khan)
   - Constitution of Pakistan 1962
   - Martial Law 1969-1970 (Yahya Khan)
   - Legal Framework Order 1970

Types of Subordinate Legislation:
   - Rules under central and provincial Acts
   - Ordinance rules and regulations
   - Martial Law Orders and Regulations (MLOs, MLRs)
   - Governor's Orders
   - Municipal and local body by-laws
   - Service rules and regulations
   - Industrial and trade regulations
   - Family law rules
   - Revenue and tax rules
   - Education and university rules
   - Cooperative society rules

4. OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

The specific objectives of the SD-1 assignment are:

   a) To create a complete and authoritative inventory of ALL delegated/
      subordinate legislations enacted during 1799-1970 applicable to
      present-day Bangladesh.

   b) To collect and preserve the full texts of all identified subordinate
      legislations.

   c) To analyze each subordinate legislation for its legal standing,
      applicability, and current status.

   d) To classify and categorize all subordinate legislations using a
      standardized framework.

   e) To prepare a structured Code of Delegated/Subordinate Legislation
      that serves as a comprehensive, accessible, and authoritative
      reference for lawmakers, judiciary, legal practitioners, government
      agencies, and the public.

   f) To identify inconsistencies, overlaps, gaps, and obsolete provisions
      within the body of subordinate legislation.

   g) To provide recommendations for legislative reform where appropriate.

5. METHODOLOGY AND APPROACH
================================================================================

Our methodology follows a five-phase approach:

PHASE 1: INCEPTION AND PLANNING (Month 1)
   - Detailed review and analysis of Terms of Reference
   - Finalization of methodology and classification framework
   - Identification and mapping of all data sources and repositories
   - Establishment of digital database structure
   - Initial stakeholder consultations
   - Submission of Inception Report

PHASE 2: IDENTIFICATION AND COLLECTION (Months 2-5)
   - Systematic search of official gazettes (1799-1970)
   - Archival research at:
     * Bangladesh Gazette archives
     * National Archives of Bangladesh
     * Bangladesh Parliament Library
     * Supreme Court Library
     * Law Commission Library
     * District Record Rooms
     * University law libraries
   - Cross-referencing with parent Acts to identify all subordinate
     legislation made thereunder
   - Creation of Master Inventory with unique reference numbers
   - Digital scanning and preservation of source documents
   - Periodic progress reports to the Project Office

PHASE 3: ANALYSIS AND CLASSIFICATION (Months 4-8)
   - Legal analysis of each identified subordinate legislation:
     * Identification of parent/enabling Act and section
     * Determination of the rule-making authority
     * Assessment of legal validity and constitutional compliance
     * Determination of current status:
       (i)   Active/In Force
       (ii)  Amended (with amendment history)
       (iii) Repealed (with repealing instrument)
       (iv)  Obsolete/Spent
       (v)   Superseded
     * Subject matter classification
     * Ministry/Division classification
   - Cross-referencing between related subordinate legislations
   - Identification of inconsistencies, conflicts, and gaps
   - Quality review of analysis

PHASE 4: CODIFICATION AND DRAFTING (Months 7-10)
   - Preparation of the Code structure and format
   - Drafting of the Code with:
     * Chronological arrangement within each category
     * Subject matter index
     * Ministry/Division index
     * Parent Act cross-reference index
     * Status index (active/repealed/amended)
     * Alphabetical index
     * Keyword search index
   - Annotation of each entry with:
     * Full citation
     * Parent Act reference
     * Amendment history
     * Current status
     * Cross-references
     * Brief synopsis
   - Internal quality review and peer verification

PHASE 5: FINALIZATION AND SUBMISSION (Months 10-12)
   - Draft Code submission to Project Office
   - Stakeholder review and consultation
   - Incorporation of feedback
   - Final quality assurance review
   - Submission of Final Code with all supporting documentation
   - Presentation to the Project Office and relevant authorities

7. CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

All identified subordinate legislations will be classified using the following
multi-dimensional framework:

DIMENSION 1: HISTORICAL ERA
   - Era 1: East India Company Period (1799-1858)
   - Era 2: British Crown Rule (1858-1947)
   - Era 3: Pakistan Period (1947-1970)

DIMENSION 2: TYPE OF INSTRUMENT
   - Rules
   - Regulations
   - By-Laws
   - Orders (Executive, Statutory, General, Special)
   - Notifications (Statutory, General)
   - Circulars
   - Schemes
   - Directions

DIMENSION 3: SUBJECT MATTER (Major Categories)
   01. Constitutional and Administrative Law
   02. Criminal Law and Justice
   03. Civil Law and Justice
   04. Land and Revenue
   05. Local Government and Municipal Affairs
   06. Education
   07. Health and Sanitation
   08. Labour and Employment
   09. Trade, Commerce and Industry
   10. Finance, Banking and Taxation
   11. Transport and Communication
   12. Agriculture, Fisheries and Livestock
   13. Forests and Environment
   14. Police and Public Order
   15. Prisons and Correctional Services
   16. Registration and Stamps
   17. Family Law and Personal Law
   18. Military and Defence
   19. Religious and Charitable Endowments
   20. Miscellaneous

DIMENSION 4: RULE-MAKING AUTHORITY
   - Governor-General in Council
   - Governor of Bengal/East Pakistan
   - Central/Federal Government
   - Provincial Government
   - Specific Ministry/Division
   - Statutory Body/Authority
   - Local Government/Municipal Authority

DIMENSION 5: CURRENT STATUS
   - Active/In Force
   - Amended (with history)
   - Repealed (with instrument)
   - Obsolete/Spent
   - Superseded
   - Partially in Force
   - Status Uncertain (requires further verification)

10. QUALITY ASSURANCE FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

   a) Source Verification: Every subordinate legislation entry will be
      verified against at least TWO independent sources.

   b) Legal Review: All analysis and classification will be reviewed by
      a senior legal professional.

   c) Peer Review: Cross-checking by team members to ensure consistency.

   d) Random Sampling: Periodic random sampling and audit of entries for
      accuracy and completeness.

   e) Stakeholder Validation: Periodic reviews by the Project Office.

   f) Final Quality Audit: Comprehensive audit before final submission.
