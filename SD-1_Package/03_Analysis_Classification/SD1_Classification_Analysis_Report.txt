ERA 1: EAST INDIA COMPANY PERIOD (1799-1858)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Overview:
The earliest period of codified subordinate legislation in Bengal dates from
1799 when the Governor-General Lord Wellesley initiated systematic regulation.
The Bengal Regulations (1799-1834) represent the foundational layer of
delegated legislation applicable to present-day Bangladesh.

Key Characteristics:
   - Regulations were the primary instrument of governance
   - Governor-General in Council held both legislative and executive power
   - Subordinate legislation was less differentiated from primary legislation
   - Revenue administration was the dominant subject area
   - Judicial administration regulations were extensive
   - Limited separation of powers

Significant Subordinate Legislations Identified:
   [To be populated with detailed entries from Master Inventory]

Analysis Notes:
   - Many regulations from this era have been repealed or superseded
   - Some foundational principles from these regulations survive in
     current law (particularly land revenue and civil procedure)
   - Language: primarily English, with some Persian/Urdu documents

ERA 2: BRITISH CROWN RULE (1858-1947)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Overview:
This is the most prolific period for subordinate legislation, spanning nearly
90 years of British Crown administration. The Government of India Acts of
1919 and 1935 significantly expanded the scope and volume of delegated
legislation, particularly at the provincial level.

Key Characteristics:
   - Clear distinction between primary and subordinate legislation
   - Extensive rule-making under major codifying statutes
   - Provincial autonomy (post-1919) led to Bengal-specific rules
   - Systematic gazette publication
   - Rapid expansion during World Wars (emergency regulations)
   - Local government by-laws proliferated

ERA 3: PAKISTAN PERIOD (1947-1970)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Overview:
Upon independence in 1947, Pakistan (including East Pakistan / present-day
Bangladesh) inherited the entire body of British Indian law. The Pakistan
period saw adaptation, modification, and creation of new subordinate
legislation to suit the new national context.

Key Characteristics:
   - Continuity of pre-partition laws through adaptation orders
   - New rule-making under Pakistani central and provincial Acts
   - Martial Law periods generated significant subordinate legislation
   - Islamic law considerations influenced some rules
   - Provincial autonomy for East Pakistan in certain subjects
   - Land reform generated new subordinate legislation

Major Parent Acts and Their Subordinate Legislations:
