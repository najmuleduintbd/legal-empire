I. INTRODUCTORY NOTE AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

LEGAL CONTINUITY FRAMEWORK:
Upon the partition of British India on 14 August 1947, the Indian Independence
Act, 1947 (10 & 11 Geo. 6. c. 30) provided under Section 18 that all existing
laws would continue in force in Pakistan until altered, repealed, or amended by
competent authority. This principle of legal continuity meant that the entire
body of British Indian subordinate legislation -- rules, regulations, by-laws,
orders, notifications -- remained operative in Pakistan (including East Bengal/
East Pakistan) from 1947 onward.

The Pakistan (Provisional Constitution) Order, 1947, adapted by the Governor-
General, further confirmed this continuity. Subsequently, the Government of
India (Adaptation of Existing Pakistani Laws) Order, 1947, made textual
adaptations (replacing "British India" with "Pakistan," "Governor-General in
Council" with "Governor-General," etc.) but preserved the substance.

For East Bengal (renamed East Pakistan in 1956), the dual structure of central
and provincial legislation meant that subordinate legislation existed at both
levels:
  - Central (Federal): Rules/regulations under Acts of the Central Legislature
    (Dominion Parliament, later National Assembly)
  - Provincial: Rules/regulations under East Bengal/East Pakistan Provincial
    Assembly Acts, as well as continued pre-partition Bengal Acts

KEY ENABLING INSTRUMENTS FOR SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION:
  1. General Clauses Act, 1897 (Act X of 1897) - Section 21: Powers of
     rule-making authorities
  2. Individual parent Acts containing specific delegation clauses
  3. Essential Services (Maintenance) Ordinance, 1952
  4. Various Adaptation Orders (1947, 1949, 1950, 1956, 1962)

PUBLICATION REQUIREMENTS:
All subordinate legislations were required to be published in:
  - The Gazette of Pakistan (for central legislation)
  - The Dhaka/Dacca Gazette (East Bengal/East Pakistan Gazette) for provincial
    subordinate legislation
  - Occasionally in the Bangladesh Gazette post-1971 when re-notified

NOTE ON CURRENT STATUS:
Under Article 149 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of Bangladesh
(1972), and by virtue of the Bangladesh (Adaptation of Existing Laws) Order,
1972 (President's Order No. 48 of 1972), known as the Laws Continuance
Enforcement Order, all Pakistan-era laws (including subordinate legislation)
continued in force in Bangladesh subject to modifications, adaptations, and
repeals made from time to time. The Bangladesh Laws Revision and Declaration
Act, 1973 (Act VIII of 1973) further confirmed this.

II. LAND REFORM RULES AND REGULATIONS
================================================================================

1.  Title: East Bengal State Acquisition and Tenancy Rules, 1951
    Year: 1951
    Type: Rules
    Parent/Enabling Act: East Bengal State Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950
        (East Bengal Act XXVIII of 1951)
    Subject: Land reform, abolition of zamindari, acquisition of rent-
        receiving interests, tenancy rights, ceiling on landholding
    Current Status: Active (with amendments) - One of the most important
        land law instruments still operative in Bangladesh
    Notes: These rules prescribed detailed procedures for:
        - Notification and acquisition of rent-receiving interests
        - Preparation of Compensation Assessment Rolls (CA Rolls)
        - Assessment and payment of compensation
        - Preparation of record of rights (khatians)
        - Revenue survey procedures
        - Transfer of holdings and mutation
        - Ceiling on holdings and redistribution of surplus land

XXII. METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
================================================================================

1. SCOPE CHALLENGES:
   The period 1947-1970 presents unique challenges because:
   - Many subordinate legislations were published only in the Gazette and
     never compiled into any single volume
   - Some Gazette issues from this period are missing or deteriorated
   - The distinction between "central" and "provincial" subordinate
     legislation requires careful sorting -- some central rules had
     differentiated application in East and West Pakistan
   - Martial law regulations (1958-1962, 1969-1970) were often issued
     without formal gazette notification

2. ADAPTATION AND CONTINUITY:
   Three layers of subordinate legislation must be tracked:
   Layer 1: Pre-1947 British Indian rules/regulations that continued under
            the legal continuity principle (these overlap with the 1799-1947
            portion of SD-1)
   Layer 2: New subordinate legislation issued 1947-1970 under new Pakistani/
            East Pakistani parent legislation
   Layer 3: Adaptation orders that modified pre-existing subordinate
            legislation

3. CURRENT STATUS DETERMINATION:
   To determine current status (Active/Amended/Repealed/Obsolete), the
   following must be checked:
   - Bangladesh (Adaptation of Existing Laws) Order, 1972 (P.O. 48 of 1972)
   - Laws Continuance Enforcement Order, 1971
   - Bangladesh Laws Revision and Declaration Act, 1973
   - Subsequent specific repeal acts
   - Specific amendment acts and ordinances
   - Bangladesh Gazette notifications of amendment/repeal
   - Bangladesh Law Commission recommendations for repeal

4. ESTIMATED VOLUME:
   Based on scholarly estimates, the total number of subordinate legislation
   instruments (rules, regulations, by-laws, orders, notifications) from
   the 1947-1970 period applicable to East Pakistan/Bangladesh likely
   numbers between 1,500 and 3,000 individual instruments. This research
   document identifies the major categories and most significant
   instruments. The full codification exercise will require systematic
   gazette-by-gazette review.

5. PRIORITY CATEGORIES FOR CODIFICATION:
   Based on continuing relevance to Bangladesh governance:
   HIGH PRIORITY (still widely operative):
   - Land and revenue rules
   - Public service rules
   - Tax rules (especially Income Tax Rules, 1962)
   - Family law rules (Muslim Family Laws Rules, 1961)
   - Labour rules
   - Court procedural rules
   - Police rules

   MEDIUM PRIORITY (partially operative or foundational):
   - Municipal by-laws
   - Education rules
   - Trade and commerce rules
   - Banking regulations
   - Transport rules

   LOWER PRIORITY (largely obsolete but needed for completeness):
   - Martial law regulations
   - Basic Democracies rules
   - Constitution-related adaptation orders
