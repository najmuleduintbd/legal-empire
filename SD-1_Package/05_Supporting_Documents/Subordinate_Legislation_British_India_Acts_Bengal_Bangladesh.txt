================================================================================
COMPREHENSIVE LIST OF SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION UNDER BRITISH INDIA ACTS (1858-1900)
APPLICABLE TO BENGAL / PRESENT-DAY BANGLADESH
================================================================================

Compiled: 15 March 2026
Scope: Rules, Regulations, Orders, Notifications, and By-Laws made under 21 parent
Acts of the British India period (1860-1899) with applicability to the Bengal
Presidency and/or the territory of present-day Bangladesh.

NOTE ON METHODOLOGY: This compilation draws on the Bangladesh Laws portal
(bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd), India Code (indiacode.nic.in), historical legal manuals
archived at archive.org and HathiTrust, the Banglapedia encyclopaedia, and
secondary legal scholarship. Where a specific subordinate instrument is known
by title and date it is listed individually; where the enabling provision is
known but individual instruments were issued ad hoc by notification (e.g.,
epidemic-disease regulations, gazetted exemption orders), the class of
instrument is described with known examples.

================================================================================
1. INDIAN PENAL CODE, 1860 (Act No. XLV of 1860)
================================================================================

The Indian Penal Code is a substantive criminal law statute. It does not itself
contain rule-making powers in the conventional sense (i.e., it does not delegate
power to the executive to make "rules" under the Act). Subordinate instruments
connected with the IPC therefore take the form of:

1.1  Government Notifications Specifying or Extending Application
     - Notification extending the IPC to Scheduled Districts and newly
       acquired territories in Bengal (various dates, 1860s-1890s).
     - Authority: Governor-General in Council / Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal.
     - Status: Spent (territories long since integrated).

1.2  Notifications under Section 294A (Lotteries)
     - Notifications by the Local Government authorising or prohibiting
       specific lotteries in Bengal.
     - Authority: Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal.
     - Status: Historical; section subsequently amended.

1.3  Notifications Declaring "Public Servants" (Section 21)
     - Various gazette notifications declaring classes of persons to be
       "public servants" for purposes of the IPC.
     - Authority: Governor-General in Council.
     - Year: Multiple, from 1860 onward.
     - Status: Superseded by subsequent legislation in Bangladesh (the Penal
       Code, 1860 remains in force in Bangladesh with amendments).

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Penal Code, 1860").
In India, replaced by Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (effective 1 July 2024).

2. POLICE ACT, 1861 (Act No. V of 1861)
================================================================================

The Police Act 1861 is the foundational police organisation statute. It confers
extensive rule-making and regulation-making powers on the Inspector-General,
the Local Government, and the District Magistrate.

2.1  POLICE REGULATIONS OF BENGAL (PRB), 1943
     - Full Title: Police Regulations, Bengal, 1943
     - Year of Promulgation: 1943 (consolidated earlier regulations dating
       from the 1860s onward)
     - Authority: Inspector-General of Police, Bengal, with the sanction of
       the Government of Bengal
     - Description: Comprehensive statutory regulations governing the
       organisation, administration, discipline, duties, and procedures of
       the Bengal Police. Contains 26 chapters and 1,290 regulations (sections).
     - Structure (Chapters):
       Chapter I    - Organisation
       Chapter II   - Relations with Other Departments
       Chapter III  - Direction and Control
       Chapter IV   - Privileges and General Instructions
       Chapter V    - Circle Inspectors
       Chapter VI   - The Police-Station
       Chapter VII  - Court Police
       Chapter VIII - Railway Police
       Chapter IX   - Criminal Investigation Department
       Chapter X    - Establishments
       Chapter XI   - Special Armed Force
       Chapter XII  - Appointment, Recruitment and Promotion
       Chapter XIII - Pay, Increments and Allowances
       Chapter XIV  - Training and Examination
       Chapter XV   - Leave, Postings and Transfers
       Chapter XVI  - Resignation, Retirement, Pension and Gratuity
       Chapter XVII - Discipline and Punishments
       Chapter XVIII- Appeals
       Chapter XIX  - Uniform and Clothing
       Chapter XX   - Arms, Ammunition and Stores
       Chapter XXI  - Buildings
       Chapter XXII - Transport
       Chapter XXIII- Accounts and Budget
       Chapter XXIV - Miscellaneous
       Chapter XXV  - Reports, Returns and Statistics
       Chapter XXVI - Furniture, Forms, Stationery and Stamps
     - Current Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (adapted post-1971).
       Also in force (with modifications) in West Bengal, India.

2.2  Earlier Police Regulations and Rules (Pre-1943)
     - Police Regulations, Bengal (original compilation, various editions
       from 1860s-1930s) - consolidated into the 1943 edition.
     - Bengal Police Manual (various editions).
     - Authority: Inspector-General of Police, Bengal.
     - Status: SUPERSEDED by the 1943 consolidation.

2.3  Rules under Section 12 (Village Police / Chaukidari)
     - Chaukidari Rules for Bengal.
     - Rules for appointment, payment, and control of village watchmen.
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal).
     - Status: SUPERSEDED by subsequent local government legislation.

2.4  Police Orders (Standing Orders)
     - Issued periodically by the Inspector-General of Police, Bengal.
     - West Bengal Police Orders (1962 series onward) continue this tradition.
     - Bangladesh Police Orders continue separately.
     - Status: Ongoing series; individual orders may be in force or superseded.

2.5  Notifications under Section 15 (Special Police Officers)
     - Notifications appointing special police officers in Bengal during
       emergencies, riots, etc.
     - Authority: District Magistrate / Local Government.
     - Status: Individual notifications spent; power continues.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh. In India, replaced state
by state (West Bengal Police Act not yet enacted; the 1861 Act remains applicable).

3. INDIAN EVIDENCE ACT, 1872 (Act No. I of 1872)
================================================================================

The Indian Evidence Act is a substantive-procedural law governing admissibility
of evidence. Like the IPC, it contains very limited rule-making delegation. The
High Court's power to make rules of procedure touching on evidentiary matters
derives from the Code of Civil Procedure and Criminal Procedure, not from the
Evidence Act itself.

3.1  No Separate Subordinate Legislation (Rules/Regulations)
     - The Evidence Act does not delegate rule-making power to the executive.
     - Procedural rules concerning the reception of evidence are contained in
       the High Court Rules (made under CPC and CrPC, not under the Evidence
       Act per se).

3.2  Government Notifications
     - Notifications under Section 78 (proof of documents by production of
       certified copies) -- Government of Bengal notifications designating
       officers authorised to certify copies.
     - Notifications under Section 86 (presumption as to certified copies of
       foreign judicial records) -- Central Government notifications.
     - Status: Superseded by subsequent notifications; power continues.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Evidence Act, 1872").
In India, replaced by Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (effective 1 July 2024).

================================================================================
4. INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 (Act No. IX of 1872)
================================================================================

The Indian Contract Act is a substantive law of obligations. It does not contain
any rule-making provision or delegation to the executive to make subordinate
legislation.

4.1  No Subordinate Legislation
     - The Act contains no section empowering the Government or any authority
       to make rules, regulations, or notifications of a legislative character.
     - All matters are dealt with substantively in the Act itself.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Contract Act, 1872").
In India, the original Act continues in force.

5. INDIAN MAJORITY ACT, 1875 (Act No. IX of 1875)
================================================================================

A short statute (only 4 substantive sections) fixing the age of majority.

5.1  No Subordinate Legislation
     - The Act does not delegate any rule-making power.
     - No rules, regulations, or notifications of legislative character were
       made under this Act.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Majority Act, 1875").
In India, the original Act continues in force.

6. SPECIFIC RELIEF ACT, 1877 (Act No. I of 1877)
================================================================================

A substantive-remedial statute providing for specific performance of contracts,
rectification, cancellation, declaratory decrees, and injunctions.

6.1  No Subordinate Legislation (Rules/Regulations)
     - The Act does not contain provisions for executive rule-making.
     - Procedural matters relating to specific relief are governed by rules of
       court made under the Code of Civil Procedure, not under this Act.

6.2  Notifications Extending Application
     - Notifications by the Governor-General in Council extending the Act to
       territories in Bengal not originally covered.
     - Status: Spent.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Specific Relief Act,
1877"). In India, replaced by the Specific Relief Act, 1963.

7. INDIAN TREASURE TROVE ACT, 1878 (Act No. VI of 1878)
================================================================================

7.1  Notifications under Section 4 (Publication of Treasure Found)
     - Individual notifications by District Collectors publishing details of
       treasure found, requiring claimants to appear.
     - Authority: Collector of the district.
     - Year: Various (ongoing under the Act).
     - Status: Individual notifications are spent upon conclusion of
       proceedings; the notification-making power continues.

7.2  Rules under Section 15 (Power to Make Rules)
     - Title: Rules under the Treasure Trove Act (Bengal)
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal)
     - Description: Rules prescribing the procedure for Collectors in dealing
       with treasure trove, forms of notification, and mode of disposal.
     - Year: Rules first framed in the 1880s; revised periodically.
     - Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (adapted). The power to make rules
       continues under the Act.

7.3  Notifications Declaring Treasure Ownerless
     - Notifications by Collectors declaring specific treasure to be ownerless
       property vesting in the Government.
     - Authority: Collector.
     - Status: Individual notifications spent; power continues.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Treasure Trove Act,
1878").

8. INDIAN ARMS ACT, 1878 (Act No. XI of 1878)
================================================================================

8.1  INDIAN ARMS RULES, 1924
     - Full Title: The Indian Arms Rules, 1924
     - Year of Promulgation: 1924 (made under sections 27 and 29 of the
       Indian Arms Act, 1878, as amended by Acts XX of 1919 and XVIII of 1920)
     - Authority: Governor-General in Council
     - Description: Comprehensive rules prescribing the forms and conditions
       for firearms licenses, the officers empowered to grant licenses,
       exemptions, fees, and schedules of prohibited and permitted arms.
     - Key Schedules:
       Schedule I   - Arms and ammunition exempted from the operation of the Act
       Schedule II  - Arms and ammunition exempted from licensing requirements
       Schedule III - Persons exempted from licensing requirements
       Schedule IV  - Withdrawal of exemptions for specified areas
     - Status: REPEALED in India by the Arms Act, 1959, and Arms Rules, 1962.
       In Bangladesh, SUPERSEDED by the Arms Act, 1878 as adapted, and
       subsequent Bangladesh arms legislation.

8.2  BENGAL ARMS ACT MANUAL, 1924 (as modified up to June 1933)
     - Full Title: Government of Bengal, Police Department, The Bengal Arms
       Act Manual, 1924, as modified up to June 1933
     - Authority: Government of Bengal, Police Department
     - Description: Departmental compilation consolidating the Arms Act, Arms
       Rules, and all Bengal-specific notifications, exemptions, circulars,
       and executive instructions relating to arms in Bengal.
     - Status: SUPERSEDED.

8.3  Notifications under Section 27 (Exemptions and Withdrawals)
     - Numerous gazette notifications by the Governor-General in Council and
       the Local Government granting or withdrawing exemptions for particular
       classes of persons, particular areas, or particular types of arms.
     - Authority: Governor-General in Council / Government of Bengal.
     - Status: SUPERSEDED by subsequent arms legislation.

8.4  Bengal-Specific Amending Legislation Affecting Arms Rules
     - Bengal Criminal Law (Arms and Explosives) Act, 1932
     - Bengal Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 1934 (Ben. Act VII of 1934)
       -- Inserted sections 19A and 20A into the Arms Act as applied to Bengal.
     - Status: REPEALED / SUPERSEDED.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Arms Act, 1878"
with amendments). In India, REPEALED by the Arms Act, 1959.

9. NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS ACT, 1881 (Act No. XXVI of 1881)
================================================================================

9.1  No Subordinate Legislation (Rules/Regulations)
     - The Negotiable Instruments Act does not contain provisions empowering
       the Government to make rules or regulations.
     - The Act is self-contained in its substantive and procedural provisions
       relating to promissory notes, bills of exchange, and cheques.

9.2  Notifications under Section 25 (Bank Holidays / Public Holidays)
     - Notifications by the Local Government declaring days to be public
       holidays for the purposes of the Act (days on which negotiable
       instruments falling due need not be paid).
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal).
     - Year: Various, issued periodically.
     - Status: Power continues; individual notifications superseded annually.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Negotiable
Instruments Act, 1881"). In India, the Act continues with amendments.

10. TRANSFER OF PROPERTY ACT, 1882 (Act No. IV of 1882)
================================================================================

10.1 No General Subordinate Legislation
     - The Transfer of Property Act does not delegate general rule-making
       power to the executive.

10.2 Notifications under Section 3 (Definition Notifications)
     - Notifications by the Local Government defining "immovable property"
       inclusions, registered/unregistered instruments, etc.
     - Status: Power continues.

10.3 Notifications under Section 54 (Transfer by Registered Instrument)
     - Notifications by the Government specifying the value threshold above
       which transfer of tangible immovable property must be by registered
       instrument.
     - Authority: Local Government.
     - Status: Various notifications; power continues.

10.4 Interaction with Bengal Tenancy Act
     - Transfer of agricultural land in Bengal was governed primarily by the
       Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885, not by the Transfer of Property Act. The
       TPA applied to non-agricultural land and buildings.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Transfer of
Property Act, 1882").

11. INDIAN TRUSTS ACT, 1882 (Act No. II of 1882)
================================================================================

11.1 No Subordinate Legislation
     - The Indian Trusts Act, 1882 does not contain any rule-making power
       delegated to the Government or any other authority.
     - The Act deals with private trusts and is entirely self-contained.

11.2 Note on Application to Bengal
     - The Trusts Act was not initially extended to the whole of Bengal;
       notifications extending the Act to various parts of Bengal were issued
       by the Governor-General in Council.
     - Status: Spent notifications; the Act now extends to all of Bangladesh.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Trusts Act, 1882").
Note: The Act was never extended to the whole of Bangladesh/East Bengal -- it
applies only where specifically extended. In India, the Act continues in force.

================================================================================
12. INDIAN EASEMENTS ACT, 1882 (Act No. V of 1882)
================================================================================

12.1 No Subordinate Legislation
     - The Indian Easements Act does not contain any provision delegating
       rule-making power to the Government or any authority.
     - The Act is substantive law dealing with easement rights and is
       entirely self-contained.

12.2 Note on Application
     - The Easements Act was not extended to the whole of British India. In
       Bengal, its application was limited. Notifications extending the Act
       to specific areas of Bengal were issued from time to time.
     - Status: The Act applies in Bangladesh to the extent of prior extensions.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Easements Act, 1882").

13. BENGAL TENANCY ACT, 1885 (Bengal Act No. VIII of 1885)
================================================================================

This Act is the most prolific generator of subordinate legislation among all the
Acts listed here. Being a provincial (Bengal) Act dealing with the complex land
tenure system, it required extensive subordinate instruments.

13.1 GOVERNMENT RULES UNDER THE BENGAL TENANCY ACT
     - Full Title: Government Rules under the Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885
     - Year: First framed 1885-1886; revised and consolidated periodically
       (notably 1889, 1898, 1920, 1935)
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal), under powers
       conferred by multiple sections of the Act
     - Description: Comprehensive rules prescribing procedures for:
       (a) Survey and settlement operations (under Chapters X and XA)
       (b) Preparation and maintenance of records-of-rights
       (c) Rent enhancement and reduction proceedings
       (d) Commutation of rents
       (e) Transfer of tenancies and pre-emption
       (f) Forms and registers for revenue officers
       (g) Procedure for Collectors in dealing with landlord-tenant disputes
     - Published in: Appendix A of the Bengal Survey and Settlement Manual, 1935
     - Status: SUPERSEDED in Bangladesh by the East Bengal State Acquisition
       and Tenancy Act, 1950, and its rules. Portions may still have residual
       applicability. In West Bengal, superseded by subsequent legislation.

13.2 HIGH COURT RULES UNDER THE BENGAL TENANCY ACT
     - Full Title: Rules of the Calcutta High Court under the Bengal Tenancy
       Act, 1885
     - Year: Various editions from 1886 onward
     - Authority: High Court at Calcutta (under sections delegating procedural
       rule-making to the High Court)
     - Description: Rules governing:
       (a) Procedure in civil courts for suits under the Act
       (b) Appeals and revisions in tenancy matters
       (c) Forms of pleadings and decrees in tenancy suits
       (d) Reference of questions to the High Court
     - Published in: Appendix III of annotated editions of the Act (Rampini,
       Finucane, and other commentators)
     - Status: SUPERSEDED by subsequent High Court rules and by the EBSAT Act, 1950.

13.3 REGISTRATION DEPARTMENT RULES
     - Full Title: Rules of the Registration Department under the Bengal
       Tenancy Act, 1885
     - Authority: Inspector-General of Registration, Bengal
     - Description: Rules prescribing:
       (a) Registration of pattas (leases) and kabooliyats (acceptances)
       (b) Registration of transfers of tenancy interests
       (c) Maintenance of registers of tenancies
     - Status: SUPERSEDED.

13.4 BOARD OF REVENUE FORMS AND INSTRUCTIONS
     - Full Title: Forms of Registers under the Bengal Tenancy Act prescribed
       by the Board of Revenue, with Board of Revenue Instructions
     - Authority: Board of Revenue, Bengal
     - Description: Prescribed forms including:
       (a) Khatian (record-of-rights) forms -- working form and final form
       (b) Khasra (field-book / plot register) forms
       (c) Statistical registers
       (d) Revenue assessment forms
     - Published in: Appendix II of annotated editions; also in Bengal Survey
       and Settlement Manual, 1935
     - Status: SUPERSEDED (forms updated under EBSAT Act, 1950).

13.5 NOTIFICATIONS EXTENDING THE ACT TO NEW AREAS
     - Notification No. 963 T.R., dated 5 November 1898: Extended the Bengal
       Tenancy Act to the whole of the Jalpaiguri district (except the Western
       Duars) with effect from 1 January 1899, subject to restrictions and
       modifications.
     - Various other notifications extending the Act to Scheduled Districts,
       Chittagong Hill Tracts, and other areas.
     - Authority: Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal.
     - Status: Spent (areas now covered by superseding legislation).

13.6 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 49 (RENT RATES)
     - Notifications fixing or revising rent rates for specific areas.
     - Authority: Local Government / Board of Revenue.
     - Status: SUPERSEDED.

13.7 BENGAL SURVEY AND SETTLEMENT MANUAL, 1935
     - Full Title: The Bengal Survey and Settlement Manual, 1935
     - Year: 1935
     - Authority: Government of Bengal, Revenue Department
     - Description: Comprehensive administrative manual consolidating all
       rules, notifications, forms, and procedures for conducting survey and
       settlement operations under the Bengal Tenancy Act and allied
       legislation. Contains:
       Part I   - General (history, legislation, organisation)
       Part II  - Survey (traverses, maps, boundaries)
       Part III - Settlement (record-of-rights, rent, attestation)
       Part IV  - Special subjects (partition, diluvion, alluvion)
       Appendix A - Government Rules under the Bengal Tenancy Act
       Appendix B - Standard Notifications (i), (ii), and (iii)
       Appendix C-O - Various supplementary materials
       Appendix X - Complete list of prescribed forms
     - Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh to the extent not superseded. Used as
       reference by the Department of Land Records and Surveys (DLRS),
       Bangladesh.

13.8 BENGAL TENANCY (AMENDMENT) NOTIFICATIONS
     - Various notifications amending schedules, rent tables, and area
       designations under the Act.
     - Authority: Government of Bengal.
     - Status: SUPERSEDED.

Current Status of Parent Act: REPEALED in Bangladesh (replaced by the East Bengal
State Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950). REPEALED in West Bengal (replaced by
the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, 1955, and other legislation).

14. INDIAN TELEGRAPH ACT, 1885 (Act No. XIII of 1885)
================================================================================

14.1 INDIAN TELEGRAPH RULES
     - Full Title: Indian Telegraph Rules (original rules framed under
       Section 7 of the Act)
     - Year: Originally framed 1885-1886; revised and consolidated as the
       Indian Telegraph Rules, 1951
     - Authority: Central Government (Governor-General in Council; later
       Government of India / Government of Bangladesh)
     - Description: Rules for the conduct of all telegraphs established,
       maintained, or worked by the Government or by licensees, including:
       (a) Conditions for establishment and working of telegraphs
       (b) Tariffs and charges for telegraph messages
       (c) Priority of messages (Government messages, press messages, etc.)
       (d) Conditions for grant of licenses
       (e) Procedure for requisitioning telegraph lines
       (f) Rules for wireless telegraphy on ships and aircraft
     - Status: The 1951 consolidation is IN FORCE in India (with amendments).
       In Bangladesh, rules adapted under the Telegraph Act as applicable.

14.2 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 4 (LICENSING)
     - Notifications by the Government granting licenses for telegraph
       operations in Bengal.
     - Authority: Central Government.
     - Status: Individual licenses spent or superseded.

14.3 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 5 (INTERCEPTION)
     - Notifications authorising interception of telegraph messages on
       grounds of public safety or emergency.
     - Authority: Central Government / authorised officers.
     - Status: Power continues; framework updated by Rule 419A of the
       Indian Telegraph Rules, 1951 (India). Bangladesh has separate
       provisions.

14.4 RULES FOR WIRELESS TELEGRAPHY
     - Indian Wireless Telegraphy (Commercial Radio Operators Certificates
       of Proficiency and Licence to Operate Wireless Telegraph) Rules, 1954
     - Indian Wireless Telegraphy (Amateur Service) Rules, 1958
     - Indian Wireless Telegraphy (Experimental Service) Rules, 1962
     - Authority: Central Government (India).
     - Note: These post-independence rules are made under the Indian Wireless
       Telegraphy Act, 1933, not directly under the 1885 Telegraph Act, but
       are part of the regulatory framework originating from the 1885 Act.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Telegraph Act, 1885").
In India, repealed by the Indian Telecommunication Act, 2023 (not yet fully
operational as of 2026).

16. CHARITABLE ENDOWMENTS ACT, 1890 (Act No. VI of 1890)
================================================================================

16.1 RULES UNDER SECTION 13
     - Full Title: Rules under the Charitable Endowments Act, 1890
     - Authority: Appropriate Government (Government of Bengal; later
       Government of Bangladesh)
     - Description: Rules for carrying out the purposes of the Act,
       including:
       (a) Procedure for vesting property in the Treasurer of Charitable
           Endowments
       (b) Terms and conditions of vesting
       (c) Accounts and audit of endowment property
       (d) Annual publication of list of vested properties in Official Gazette
       (e) Mode of application for vesting orders and schemes
     - Year: Rules first framed after 1890; revised periodically.
     - Requirement: All rules must be published in the Official Gazette
       (requirement added by amendment to ensure subordinate legislation
       transparency).
     - Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (with amendments). The rules are
       adapted to Bangladesh conditions.

16.2 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 4 (VESTING ORDERS)
     - Full Title: Notifications vesting property in the Treasurer of
       Charitable Endowments
     - Authority: Appropriate Government.
     - Description: Individual notifications published in the Official Gazette
       ordering that specified charitable properties be vested in the Treasurer.
     - Status: Individual notifications in force unless property subsequently
       divested.

16.3 BENGAL-SPECIFIC AMENDMENTS AFFECTING SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION
     - Bengal Wakf Act, 1934 (Bengal Act XIII of 1934):
       -- Section 79 added a proviso to Section 5 applicable only to Bengal,
          excluding wakf properties from the Charitable Endowments Act.
       -- Section 80 added a new sub-section (3) to Section 5, applicable
          only to Bengal, providing for separate administration of wakf
          endowments.
     - Status: These Bengal-specific amendments are IN FORCE in Bangladesh
       through the Waqf Ordinance, 1962, and its successors.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Charitable
Endowments Act, 1890").

17. LAND ACQUISITION ACT, 1894 (Act No. I of 1894)
================================================================================

17.1 BENGAL LAND ACQUISITION MANUAL, 1917
     - Full Title: The Bengal Land Acquisition Manual, 1917
     - Year: 1917
     - Authority: Government of Bengal, Revenue Department
     - Description: Comprehensive departmental manual consolidating all rules,
       procedures, forms, and executive instructions for land acquisition in
       Bengal under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894. Covers:
       (a) Procedure for preliminary investigation (Section 4 notifications)
       (b) Declaration of intended acquisition (Section 6)
       (c) Measurement, marking out, and plans
       (d) Procedure for assessment of compensation
       (e) Reference to civil courts
       (f) Rules for temporary occupation
       (g) Acquisition for companies
       (h) Forms and returns
     - Status: SUPERSEDED in Bangladesh by the Acquisition and Requisition
       of Immovable Property Ordinance, 1982.

17.2 RULES UNDER SECTION 55 (POWER TO MAKE RULES)
     - Full Title: Rules under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 (Bengal)
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal)
     - Description: Rules prescribing:
       (a) The manner of giving notice under Section 4 (preliminary
           notification)
       (b) The mode of making declarations under Section 6
       (c) Procedure for the Collector's enquiry
       (d) Scales of compensation and solatium
       (e) Procedure for reference to the court
       (f) Appeals
     - Status: SUPERSEDED in Bangladesh (see 17.1 above). In West Bengal,
       superseded by the West Bengal Land Acquisition Manual, 1991 (revised 2006).

17.3 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 4 (PRELIMINARY NOTIFICATIONS)
     - Individual notifications by Collectors giving public notice that land
       in a particular locality is needed or is likely to be needed for a
       public purpose.
     - Authority: Collector (on behalf of the appropriate Government).
     - Status: Individual notifications spent on acquisition or lapse.

17.4 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 6 (DECLARATIONS)
     - Formal declarations that specified land is needed for a public purpose,
       published in the Official Gazette.
     - Authority: Appropriate Government.
     - Status: Individual notifications spent.

17.5 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 40 (ACQUISITION FOR COMPANIES)
     - Notifications by the appropriate Government directing acquisition of
       land for a company (private entity).
     - Authority: Appropriate Government.
     - Status: Individual notifications spent.

Current Status of Parent Act: REPEALED in Bangladesh (replaced by the Acquisition
and Requisition of Immovable Property Ordinance, 1982, later replaced by the
Immovable Property Acquisition and Requisition Act, 2017). In India, replaced
by the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition,
Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013.

18. GENERAL CLAUSES ACT, 1897 (Act No. X of 1897)
================================================================================

18.1 No Subordinate Legislation
     - The General Clauses Act is an interpretive statute providing general
       definitions and rules of construction for all legislation. It does not
       itself delegate any rule-making power and no subordinate legislation
       is made under it.
     - The Act defines terms such as "Government Gazette," "notification,"
       "rule," etc. that are used throughout all other legislation.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The General Clauses
Act, 1897").

19. EPIDEMIC DISEASES ACT, 1897 (Act No. III of 1897)
================================================================================

19.1 TEMPORARY REGULATIONS UNDER SECTION 2
     - Title: Regulations prescribed under Section 2 of the Epidemic Diseases
       Act (various)
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal; later Government of
       Bangladesh)
     - Description: When the Government is satisfied that any part of the
       territory is visited by or threatened with an outbreak of dangerous
       epidemic disease, it may prescribe temporary regulations including:
       (a) Inspection of persons travelling by railway or otherwise
       (b) Segregation of persons suspected of infection
       (c) Prohibition or regulation of fairs, gatherings, pilgrimages
       (d) Closure of schools, markets, and public places
       (e) Compulsory vaccination or inoculation measures
       (f) Quarantine measures
       (g) Any other measure to prevent the spread of disease
     - Known Historical Examples (Bengal):
       -- Plague Regulations, Bengal (various years from 1898 onward)
       -- Cholera Regulations, Bengal (various years)
       -- Smallpox Prevention Regulations
     - Year: Issued ad hoc during epidemic outbreaks from 1897 onward.
     - Status: Individual regulations are SPENT (temporary by nature). The
       power to make such regulations continues and has been exercised
       extensively, including during the COVID-19 pandemic (2020-2022).

19.2 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 2A (CENTRAL GOVERNMENT POWERS)
     - Notifications by the Central Government prescribing regulations for
       inspection of ships, vessels, and aircraft, and detention of persons
       intending to travel.
     - Authority: Central Government.
     - Status: Power continues.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Epidemic Diseases
Act, 1897").

20. CODE OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE, 1898 (Act No. V of 1898)
================================================================================

The CrPC 1898 is one of the most prolific generators of subordinate legislation,
as it empowers both the Government and the High Court to make extensive rules.

20.1 CRIMINAL RULES AND ORDERS (HIGH COURT)
     - Full Title: Criminal Rules and Orders of the High Court (various
       editions)
     - For Bengal: Criminal Rules and Orders of the High Court at Calcutta
       (pre-1947); Criminal Rules and Orders of the High Court of East
       Pakistan / High Court of Bangladesh (post-1947/1971)
     - Authority: High Court, under Sections 554 and other enabling
       provisions of the CrPC
     - Description: Comprehensive rules governing:
       (a) Practice and procedure in criminal courts subordinate to the
           High Court
       (b) Forms of charges, warrants, summonses, bonds, etc.
       (c) Procedure for appeals and revisions
       (d) Bail procedures
       (e) Procedure for recording evidence
       (f) Sessions trial procedure
       (g) Procedure for subordinate courts
       (h) Inspection of registers and records
       (i) Returns and statements to be submitted by subordinate courts
       (j) Rules for disposal of property
       (k) Procedure in habeas corpus matters
     - Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as updated by the High Court Division,
       Supreme Court of Bangladesh). In West Bengal, superseded by rules under
       the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.

20.2 RULES UNDER SECTION 554 (GENERAL RULE-MAKING POWER OF HIGH COURT)
     - The High Court may make rules for:
       (a) The form in which charges shall be framed
       (b) The procedure to be followed in the trial of warrant cases
       (c) Any matter relating to the practice and procedure of criminal
           courts subordinate to it
     - These rules form part of the Criminal Rules and Orders (see 20.1).

20.3 GOVERNMENT NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 9 (COURTS OF SESSION)
     - Notifications constituting courts of session, appointing sessions
       divisions, and specifying places for holding sessions.
     - Authority: Local Government / Provincial Government.
     - Status: Superseded by subsequent notifications; power continues.

20.4 GOVERNMENT NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 10 (ASSISTANT SESSIONS JUDGES)
     - Notifications appointing Assistant Sessions Judges.
     - Authority: Local Government.
     - Status: Individual notifications superseded.

20.5 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 14 (MAGISTRATES)
     - Notifications by the Local Government specifying classes of
       Magistrates in each district.
     - Authority: Local Government.
     - Status: System substantially reformed; individual notifications
       superseded.

20.6 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 144 (PROHIBITORY ORDERS)
     - Orders by District Magistrates and other Executive Magistrates
       prohibiting certain acts to prevent danger to human life, health,
       safety, or public tranquility.
     - Authority: District Magistrate / Sub-Divisional Magistrate.
     - Status: Power continues; individual orders temporary (up to 2 months,
       extendable to 6 months).

20.7 RULES FOR POLICE DIARIES AND CASE DIARIES
     - Rules prescribing the form and manner of maintaining police diaries
       (Section 172) and case diaries during investigation.
     - Authority: State Government / Local Government.
     - Status: IN FORCE (as part of police procedural rules).

20.8 RULES AND NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 197 (SANCTION FOR PROSECUTION)
     - Notifications specifying the authorities competent to grant sanction
       for prosecution of public servants.
     - Authority: Central/Local Government.
     - Status: Power continues.

20.9 RULES UNDER SECTION 477 (DESTRUCTION OF RECORDS)
     - Rules prescribing the period after which records of criminal cases may
       be destroyed.
     - Authority: High Court / Local Government.
     - Status: IN FORCE.

20.10 RULES UNDER SECTION 561 (FORMS)
      - Rules prescribing forms for use in criminal proceedings -- summonses,
        warrants, bonds, search warrants, etc.
      - Authority: High Court.
      - Published in: Schedule II of the CrPC and supplemented by High Court
        criminal rules.
      - Status: IN FORCE.

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Code of Criminal
Procedure, 1898"). In India, replaced by the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973,
and subsequently by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.

21. INDIAN STAMP ACT, 1899 (Act No. II of 1899)
================================================================================

21.1 STAMP RULES (UNDER SECTION 74)
     - Full Title: Rules under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (Bengal) /
       Stamp Rules
     - Authority: Local Government (Government of Bengal; later Government of
       Bangladesh)
     - Description: Rules prescribing:
       (a) Supply, distribution, and sale of stamps (adhesive and impressed)
       (b) Appointment and regulation of stamp vendors / licensed vendors
       (c) Fees for adjudication by Collectors
       (d) Procedure for adjudication of stamp duty
       (e) Mode of cancellation of adhesive stamps
       (f) Allowances and refunds for spoiled or misused stamps
       (g) Procedure for compounding offences
       (h) Forms and returns
       (i) Fines for breach of rules (not exceeding 500 rupees/taka)
     - Requirement: All rules must be published in the Official Gazette.
     - Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (with amendments). In West Bengal,
       separate stamp rules apply.

21.2 RULES FOR SUPPLY AND DISTRIBUTION OF STAMPS
     - Full Title: Rules for Supply and Distribution of Stamps (Government
       of India Rules read with Supplementary Rules framed by the
       Provincial/State Government)
     - Authority: Central Government (supply and printing) / Local Government
       (distribution)
     - Description: Rules governing:
       (a) Printing of stamps by the Controller of Stamps
       (b) Indent and supply to provincial stamp stores
       (c) Distribution to treasuries and sub-treasuries
       (d) Sale through licensed vendors
       (e) Custody and accounting of stamp stock
     - Status: IN FORCE (adapted for Bangladesh; India has separate rules).

21.3 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 9 (REDUCTION/REMISSION OF DUTIES)
     - Notifications by the Local Government reducing, remitting, or
       compounding duties payable on specified instruments.
     - Authority: Local Government.
     - Status: Individual notifications; power continues.

21.4 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SCHEDULE I (STAMP DUTY RATES)
     - Amendments to Schedule I specifying the duties chargeable on various
       instruments (conveyances, mortgages, leases, bonds, affidavits,
       agreements, powers of attorney, etc.).
     - Authority: Legislature (through amending Acts) and Government (through
       notifications where empowered).
     - Status: Schedule regularly updated by Finance Acts and notifications.

21.5 NOTIFICATIONS UNDER SECTION 3 (INSTRUMENTS CHARGEABLE)
     - Notifications specifying instruments chargeable with duty and the
       applicable rates.
     - Authority: Government.
     - Status: Power continues.

21.6 COLLECTOR'S ADJUDICATION RULES
     - Rules and standing orders governing the procedure followed by
       Collectors when adjudicating stamp duty on instruments brought before
       them under Sections 31-32.
     - Authority: Board of Revenue / Local Government.
     - Status: IN FORCE (as adapted).

Current Status of Parent Act: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as "The Stamp Act, 1899").
In India, the Indian Stamp Act continues alongside state-level stamp
legislation; the Indian Stamp (Amendment) Act, 2019 modernised certain
provisions.

NOTES AND CAVEATS
================================================================================

1. COMPLETENESS: This compilation covers all known classes of subordinate
   legislation under the listed Acts. Individual gazette notifications
   (of which there were thousands over the period 1858-1947) are not
   individually enumerable without access to complete runs of the Calcutta
   Gazette and the Gazette of India.

2. BANGLADESH ADAPTATION: After independence in 1971, Bangladesh adopted all
   pre-existing laws through the Bangladesh Laws (Revision and Declaration)
   Act, 1973 (Act VIII of 1973). This Act systematically replaced references
   to "Pakistan," "Central Government," "Provincial Government," and "Rs"
   with "Bangladesh," "Government," and "Tk" respectively.

3. INDIA REPLACEMENT: Several parent Acts have been replaced in India by
   new legislation (e.g., IPC by BNS 2023, CrPC 1898 by CrPC 1973 and
   then BNSS 2023, Specific Relief Act 1877 by SRA 1963, Arms Act 1878
   by Arms Act 1959). The subordinate legislation under the old Acts has
   been correspondingly superseded in India.

4. ARCHIVAL SOURCES: The most comprehensive historical collections of
   subordinate legislation are found in:
   - The Calcutta Gazette (official gazette of Bengal)
   - The Gazette of India
   - Annotated editions of individual Acts (e.g., Rampini's Bengal Tenancy
     Act, the Bengal Arms Act Manual)
   - The Bengal Survey and Settlement Manual, 1935
   - The Bengal Land Acquisition Manual, 1917
   - The Police Regulations, Bengal, 1943
   - The Indian Culture Portal (indianculture.gov.in)
   - The National Digital Library of India (ndl.iitkgp.ac.in)
   - Internet Archive / HathiTrust digital collections

5. ACTS WITHOUT SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION: Several Acts in this list (Indian
   Contract Act, Indian Majority Act, Indian Trusts Act, Indian Easements
   Act, General Clauses Act) are purely substantive or interpretive statutes
   that do not delegate rule-making power to the executive and therefore
   have no subordinate legislation.
