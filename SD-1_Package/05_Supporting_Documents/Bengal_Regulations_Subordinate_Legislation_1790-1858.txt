MADE UNDER BENGAL REGULATIONS AND EARLY LEGISLATION (1790-1858)
APPLICABLE TO THE TERRITORY OF PRESENT-DAY BANGLADESH

Compiled: 15 March 2026
Research Note: This compilation draws on the India Code (indiacode.nic.in),

================================================================================
PART I: THE CORNWALLIS CODE - BENGAL REGULATIONS OF 1793
(48 Regulations promulgated 1 May 1793)
================================================================================

The Cornwallis Code comprised 48 regulations spanning 387 printed pages,
covering judicial organization, revenue administration, police functions,
and civil service conduct. These formed the foundation of British administration
in Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. REVENUE AND LAND SETTLEMENT REGULATIONS (1793)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Bengal Regulation I of 1793
   Short Title: The Bengal Permanent Settlement Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Description: Enacted the articles of the Proclamation relative to the
   limitation of the public demand upon lands; declared the Permanent
   Settlement with zamindars, independent talukdars and other proprietors
   of land in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
   Status: Mostly REPEALED in India (various repealing acts); provisions
   relating to Permanent Settlement were effectively superseded by
   post-independence land reform legislation in Bangladesh (State
   Acquisition and Tenancy Act, 1950).

2. Bengal Regulation II of 1793
   Short Title: The Bengal Land-Revenue Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Description: Rules for the collection of land-revenue from zamindars and
   other landholders; established procedures for revenue collection.
   Status: Largely REPEALED / SUPERSEDED by subsequent revenue legislation.

3. Bengal Regulation VIII of 1793
   Short Title: The Bengal Decennial Settlement Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Description: Re-enacted with modifications the rules for the Decennial
   Settlement of public revenue payable from lands, originally passed for
   Bengal (18 Sept 1789), Bihar (25 Nov 1789), and Orissa (10 Feb 1790).
   Status: REPEALED in India; superseded in Bangladesh by subsequent
   land reform legislation.

4. Bengal Regulation X of 1793
   Short Title: Bengal Revenue-Free Lands (Badshahi Grants) Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Description: Rules regarding lands held revenue-free under Badshahi
   (Imperial/Mughal) grants; established procedures for verification
   and continuation of such grants.
   Status: Partially IN FORCE in some jurisdictions; largely superseded.

5. Bengal Regulation XIX of 1793
   Short Title: Bengal Revenue-Free Lands (Non-Badshahi Grants) Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Description: Rules regarding title to lands exempt from revenue other
   than Badshahi grants; established procedure for resumption proceedings.
   Status: Partially IN FORCE; largely superseded.

6. Bengal Regulation XI of 1793
   Short Title: The Bengal Inheritance Regulation, 1793
   Year: 1793 (1 May)
   Gazette: N/A (pre-Gazette era)
   Description: Removed restrictions on the operation of Hindu and
   Muhammadan laws regarding inheritance of revenue-paying landed
   property; declared native laws of inheritance applicable.
   Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (bdlaws reference); partially
   in force in Indian states.

   Subordinate Rules/Orders Known:
   - No specific subordinate rules identified; the Regulation itself
     directed courts to apply Hindu and Muhammadan personal law in
     matters of inheritance.

7. Bengal Regulation XIV of 1793
   Short Title: Revenue Sale Law (part), 1793
   Year: 1793
   Description: Rules for sale of defaulting zamindars' estates for
   arrears of revenue; established auction procedures.
   Status: REPEALED; superseded by Bengal Regulation VII of 1799 and
   subsequent revenue sale laws.

8. Bengal Regulation XVI of 1793
   Year: 1793
   Description: Provided for reference by courts to arbitration with
   consent of parties in suits for accounts, partnership debts, and
   breach of contract.
   Status: REPEALED by subsequent civil procedure legislation.

9. Bengal Regulation XXXVII of 1793
   Year: 1793
   Description: Land ownership records and registration of changes in
   land title.
   Status: REPEALED / SUPERSEDED.

10. Bengal Regulation XLVIII of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Listed in the Fourth Schedule of the Laws Local Extent
    Act, 1874; specific subject not identified in available sources.
    Status: REPEALED by Act 12 of 1891.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. JUDICIAL AND CIVIL COURT REGULATIONS (1793)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. Bengal Regulation III of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Reorganized the working of civil courts (Diwani Adalats);
    established Mofussil Diwani Adalat in each district with a civil
    servant as Judge in place of Collector.
    Status: REPEALED by subsequent judiciary acts.

12. Bengal Regulation IV of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Establishment of Provincial Courts of Appeal at Dhaka,
    Calcutta, Murshidabad, and Patna for civil cases.
    Status: REPEALED by subsequent judiciary acts.

13. Bengal Regulation V of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Further provisions regarding Provincial Courts of Appeal;
    establishment of appellate jurisdiction.
    Status: REPEALED.

14. Bengal Regulation VI of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Constitution of the Sadar Diwani Adalat (chief civil
    appellate court) at the Presidency.
    Status: REPEALED (altered by Regulation II of 1801 and subsequently
    replaced by High Court).

15. Bengal Regulation VII of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Created a regular legal profession for the Company's
    Courts; provided for the appointment of vakils (native pleaders)
    in the Courts of Civil Judicature.
    Status: REPEALED; superseded by Bengal Regulation XXVII of 1814
    and later Legal Practitioners Acts.

    Subordinate Orders:
    - Rules for admission and conduct of vakils were prescribed under
      this regulation by the Sadar Diwani Adalat.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. CRIMINAL COURTS AND POLICE REGULATIONS (1793)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16. Bengal Regulation IX of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Consolidated regulations regarding criminal courts
    (Nizamat Adalats); established the Sadar Nizamat Adalat and
    circuit courts.
    Status: REPEALED by subsequent criminal procedure legislation.

17. Bengal Regulation XXII of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Police reform regulations; division of districts into
    thanas, appointment of darogahs under district magistrates;
    abolished zamindari police system.
    Status: REPEALED; superseded by Police Act, 1861.

    Subordinate Orders:
    - Standing orders for thana darogahs regarding arrest procedures
    - Instructions for sipahi deployment under darogahs
    - Reporting requirements to district magistrates

18. Bengal Regulation XXVIII of 1793
    Year: 1793
    Description: Declared equality before law; enabled natives to file
    cases against Company officials and against the government itself.
    Status: REPEALED; principles incorporated into subsequent legislation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. OTHER REGULATIONS OF 1793
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

19-48. [Additional Regulations]
    The remaining regulations of 1793 covered matters including:
    - Regulation of stamp duties
    - Revenue collection procedures
    - Court fees
    - Prohibition of loans by covenanted servants
    - Maintenance of public roads and bridges
    - Rules for collectors
    - Administration of Hindu and Muhammadan personal law
    - Appointment of ameens (native commissioners)
    - Rules for execution of decrees
    - Registration of deeds and documents
    - Management of wards and minors
    - Salt and opium department regulations

    Status: Most REPEALED by various acts between 1837 and 1922.

================================================================================
PART III: CHARTER ACTS AND THEIR SUBORDINATE INSTRUMENTS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. CHARTER ACT OF 1793 (33 Geo. III, c. 52)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full Title: An Act for continuing in the East India Company, for a further
term, the possession of the British territories in India.
Year: 1793
Description: Extended the Company's trade monopoly by 20 years; required
payment of GBP 500,000 annually to the British Government from Indian
revenue; separated revenue administration from judicial functions.

Subordinate Instruments:
- The Cornwallis Code of 48 Regulations (see Part I above) was the
  primary legislative response to the powers granted under this Act.
- Governor-General's orders regarding separation of revenue and
  judicial functions
- Instructions regarding Company's trade operations
Status: SPENT / SUPERSEDED by Charter Act of 1813.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. CHARTER ACT OF 1813 (53 Geo. III, c. 155)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full Title: An Act for continuing in the East India Company, for a further
term, the possession of the British territories in India.
Year: 1813
Description: Ended EIC trade monopoly (except tea and China trade); allowed
Christian missionaries to enter British India; allocated funds for education
(GBP 100,000 annually); asserted Crown sovereignty over British India.

Subordinate Instruments:
- Board of Control regulations regarding missionary activities
- Educational fund allocation orders
- Trade licensing rules for private merchants
- Regulations regarding religious establishments
Status: SPENT / SUPERSEDED by Charter Act of 1833.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. CHARTER ACT OF 1833 (3 & 4 Will. IV, c. 85)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full Title: An Act for effecting an Arrangement with the East India Company,
and for the better Government of His Majesty's Indian Territories.
Year: 1833
Description: Renamed Governor-General of Bengal as Governor-General of India;
gave exclusive legislative powers to Governor-General in Council; ended EIC
commercial activities entirely; added a Law Member to the Council (first
appointee: Lord Macaulay); removed legislative powers of Bombay and Madras
Presidencies.

Subordinate Instruments:
- Order establishing the Indian Law Commission (1834), chaired by
  Lord Macaulay, to codify Indian law
- Notification appointing Law Member to Governor-General's Council
- Orders regarding transfer of legislative power from Presidency
  governments
- Instructions regarding cessation of EIC commercial operations
- Lex Loci Report (1840) and various law commission reports
Status: SUPERSEDED by Charter Act of 1853 and Government of India Act, 1858.

================================================================================
PART IV: ACTS OF THE GOVERNOR-GENERAL IN COUNCIL (1834-1858)
APPLICABLE TO BENGAL
================================================================================

After the Charter Act of 1833, legislation took the form of "Acts" passed
by the Governor-General in Council rather than "Bengal Regulations."
Key Acts applicable to Bengal territory (present-day Bangladesh):

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

94. Act I of 1834 (Governor General Act, 1834)
    Year: 1834
    Description: First Act under the new legislative arrangement
    established by the Charter Act of 1833.
    Status: REPEALED.

95. Act XXIX of 1837
    Year: 1837
    Description: Abolished Persian as the official language of courts
    and administration in Bengal; substituted vernacular languages and
    English.
    Status: SUPERSEDED.

96. Act XVI of 1843
    Year: 1843
    Description: Repeal of part of Bengal Regulations IX of 1808 and
    XVI of 1810; consolidation measure.
    Status: REPEALING ACT.

97. Act V of 1843 (Indian Slavery Act)
    Year: 1843
    Description: Abolished slavery in British India; applicable throughout
    Bengal Presidency including present-day Bangladesh.
    Status: REPEALED as spent; but the prohibition on slavery continues
    under constitutional provisions.

98. Act XXI of 1850 (Caste Disabilities Removal Act)
    Year: 1850
    Description: Provided that no person shall be deprived of rights
    of property by reason of renouncing their religion or caste.
    Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh.

99. Various Acts 1834-1858
    Description: Numerous Acts were passed during this period on subjects
    including criminal law, civil procedure, revenue administration,
    police, customs, stamps, registration, and other matters applicable
    to Bengal. Many were subsequently consolidated into major
    codifications (Indian Penal Code 1860, Code of Criminal Procedure
    1861/1882/1898, Code of Civil Procedure 1859/1877/1908, etc.).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. REGULATING ACT OF 1773 (13 Geo. III, c. 63)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Year: 1773
Description: First Parliamentary act to regulate East India Company affairs
in India; established Governor-General and Council of Bengal; created
Supreme Court at Calcutta.
Status: SUPERSEDED by subsequent Charter Acts.

Subordinate Instruments:
- Letters Patent establishing the Supreme Court of Judicature at
  Fort William, Bengal (1774)
- Rules of the Supreme Court
- Governor-General's Council orders

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. PITT'S INDIA ACT OF 1784 (24 Geo. III, c. 25)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Year: 1784
Description: Established dual control system - Board of Control in London
alongside EIC's Court of Directors; separated political and commercial
functions.
Status: SUPERSEDED by Charter Act of 1833.

Subordinate Instruments:
- Board of Control orders and dispatches to Bengal Government
- Secret Committee instructions
- Revenue dispatch regulations

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. ACT OF SETTLEMENT (1781) - 21 Geo. III, c. 70
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Year: 1781
Description: Defined and limited jurisdiction of the Supreme Court at
Calcutta; clarified its relationship with Company's courts; known as the
"Declaratory Act" or "Act of Settlement."
Status: SUPERSEDED.

D. PRE-1793 BENGAL REGULATIONS (1772-1792)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Various regulations were passed before the Cornwallis Code, forming its
precursors:

100. Regulation of 1772 (Warren Hastings)
     Description: Established the system of Diwani and Nizamat Adalats;
     Plan for administration of justice.
     Status: SUPERSEDED by Cornwallis Code of 1793.

101. Regulations of 1780-1790
     Description: Various ad hoc regulations on revenue, police, and
     judicial matters that were consolidated into the Cornwallis Code.
     Status: SUPERSEDED and incorporated into the 1793 Regulations.

NOTES AND CAVEATS
================================================================================

1. GAZETTE NOTIFICATION NUMBERS: The formal Gazette of India was not
   established until 1858. Prior to that, regulations were published
   as separate proclamations and in Company records. Therefore, gazette
   notification numbers are not available for pre-1858 legislation.

2. COMPLETENESS: This compilation identifies approximately 100 items
   of parent legislation and their known subordinate instruments. The
   original Cornwallis Code contained 48 regulations, and numerous
   additional regulations were passed between 1794 and 1834. A truly
   exhaustive list would require access to the original Bengal Code
   volumes held in the India Office Records (British Library), the
   National Archives of India, and the National Archives of Bangladesh.

3. SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION: Formal "subordinate legislation" in the
   modern sense (statutory rules, statutory orders) did not exist as
   a category in the pre-1858 period. Executive instructions took the
   form of Board of Revenue circulars, Governor-General's orders,
   administrative instructions, and standing orders. These were not
   systematically published in any gazette until much later.

4. BANGLADESH APPLICABILITY: The territory of present-day Bangladesh
   formed part of the Bengal Presidency (specifically the "Lower
   Provinces" or "Bengal Division"). After 1947, East Pakistan
   (later Bangladesh) inherited applicable pre-independence legislation.
   The Bangladesh Laws (Revision and Declaration) Act, 1973 formally
   declared which laws remained in force.

5. CURRENCY OF STATUS: The "status" indicated for each regulation
   reflects the best available information as of the date of
   compilation. Some regulations listed as "in force" may have been
   effectively superseded even if not formally repealed.
