1. CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE, 1908 (Act V of 1908)
   -- CIVIL RULES AND ORDERS
================================================================================

1.1  Bengal Civil Rules and Orders (Practice and Procedure of Civil Courts)
     Year: 1908 onwards (consolidated periodically; major editions c.1914, 1936)
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta / Government of Bengal
     Description: Comprehensive rules governing all civil court procedure in
       Bengal, covering institution of suits, service of process, interlocutory
       applications, trial, judgment, execution of decrees, appeals, revision,
       and review. Organized in multiple volumes with prescribed forms.
     Status: PARTIALLY IN FORCE in Bangladesh. Adapted for East Pakistan and
       subsequently Bangladesh. Continues to govern civil court practice.

1.2  High Court Civil Circular Orders (Bengal)
     Year: Various (1862-1947)
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta
     Description: Practice directions and administrative circulars issued to
       subordinate civil courts covering interpretation of CPC provisions,
       standard procedures, filing requirements, and case management.
     Status: Historical; many principles incorporated into later practice.

1.3  Rules for Pleaders, Mukhtars, and Revenue Agents (Bengal)
     Year: Various from 1908 onwards
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta
     Description: Rules governing appearance of legal practitioners in civil
       courts, qualifications, enrollment, conduct, fees, and discipline.
     Status: Superseded by Legal Practitioners and Bar Council legislation.

1.4  Rules for Small Cause Courts (Bengal)
     Year: 1908 onwards
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta / GoB
     Description: Special procedural rules for Provincial Small Cause Courts
       under CPC provisions, covering summary trial of civil suits below
       prescribed pecuniary limits.
     Status: Partially in force (as adapted).

1.5  Insolvency Rules (Bengal) -- under CPC provisions
     Year: Various
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta / GoB
     Description: Rules governing insolvency proceedings under the Provincial
       Insolvency Act, 1920 (cross-referenced with CPC execution provisions).
       Covers adjudication of insolvency, administration of estates,
       composition, discharge.
     Status: Partially in force (as adapted).

1.6  Commission Rules under CPC (Bengal)
     Year: 1908 onwards
     Authority: High Court of Calcutta
     Description: Rules for issuing commissions for examination of witnesses,
       local investigation, scientific investigation, and sale of property.
     Status: Partially in force.

1.7  Rules for Execution of Foreign Decrees (Bengal)
     Year: Various
     Authority: GoI / HC
     Description: Rules governing reciprocal enforcement of decrees from
       courts outside Bengal.
     Status: Historical; superseded by subsequent arrangements.

2. REGISTRATION ACT, 1908 (Act XVI of 1908)
   -- REGISTRATION RULES
================================================================================

2.1  Bengal Registration Rules, 1909
     Year: 1909 (amended periodically through 1947)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Inspector General of Registration)
     Description: Comprehensive rules for registration of documents including:
       - Compulsory and optional registration procedures
       - Duties of Sub-Registrars and Registrars
       - Maintenance of books, indexes, and registers (Books I-IV)
       - Fees for registration services
       - Procedure for presenting documents
       - Endorsements and certificates
       - Copying and search procedures
       - Filing of testamentary documents
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Registration Act 1908 and
       Bengal Registration Rules continue to govern document registration.

2.2  Registration Fee Schedule (Bengal) -- Notifications
     Year: Various notifications from 1909 onwards
     Authority: Government of Bengal
     Description: Fee schedules for registration of different classes of
       documents, periodically revised by notification.
     Status: Superseded by subsequent fee notifications but framework continues.

2.3  Rules for Maintenance of Registration Office Books and Indexes
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: Inspector General of Registration, Bengal
     Description: Detailed rules for maintenance of Book I (Non-testamentary
       documents relating to immovable property), Book II (Non-testamentary
       documents not relating to immovable property), Book III (Wills and
       authorities to adopt), Book IV (Miscellaneous documents), and Indexes.
     Status: Partially in force.

2.4  Rules for Registration of Documents by Purdanashin Women
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Special procedures for presentation and execution of
       documents by women observing purdah, including attendance at residence.
     Status: Historical; procedures modified post-independence.

3. INDIAN PORTS ACT, 1908 (Act XV of 1908)
   -- PORT RULES
================================================================================

3.1  Chittagong Port Rules
     Year: 1908 onwards (revised periodically)
     Authority: Government of Bengal / Port Officer, Chittagong
     Description: Rules governing the administration of Chittagong Port,
       covering pilotage, anchorage, berthing, loading and unloading of cargo,
       harbor safety, navigation channels, quarantine procedures, port dues,
       dangerous goods handling, and ship surveys.
     Status: Superseded by Chittagong Port Authority (CPA) rules but
       foundational framework. Ports Act 1908 continues in Bangladesh.

3.2  Rules for Minor Ports in Bengal (Narayanganj, Khulna, Chandpur, etc.)
     Year: Various from 1908 onwards
     Authority: Government of Bengal
     Description: Rules for smaller riverine ports in Bengal, covering vessel
       registration, docking, safety, and tolls.
     Status: Historical; port administration reorganized post-independence.

3.3  Pilotage Rules (Bengal Ports)
     Year: 1908 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Port Officer
     Description: Rules for appointment and duties of harbor pilots,
       compulsory pilotage areas, pilot fees, vessel navigation in port waters.
     Status: Adapted under Chittagong Port Authority framework.

3.4  Port Health Rules (Bengal)
     Year: 1908 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Port Health Officer
     Description: Quarantine procedures for incoming vessels, medical
       inspection of passengers and crew, prevention of epidemic disease
       transmission through ports.
     Status: Framework continued; adapted under modern health legislation.

4. BENGAL EXCISE ACT, 1909 (Bengal Act V of 1909)
   -- EXCISE RULES
================================================================================

4.1  Bengal Excise Rules (General)
     Year: 1909 onwards (comprehensive revision 1925, 1937)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Excise Department)
     Description: Comprehensive rules governing the manufacture, possession,
       sale, transport, import, and export of all excisable articles including
       country spirit, foreign liquor, beer, wine, and intoxicating drugs.
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Bengal Excise Act 1909
       continues to be the primary excise legislation.

4.2  Bengal Distillery Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB (Excise Commissioner)
     Description: Rules governing establishment and operation of distilleries,
       distillation processes, storage, duty payment, wastage allowance,
       record-keeping, and inspection.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

4.3  Bengal Country Spirit Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for manufacture, wholesale, and retail sale of country
       spirit (indigenous liquor); licensing of shops; minimum and maximum
       pricing; quality standards.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

4.4  Bengal Foreign Liquor Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for import, wholesale and retail of foreign liquor
       (imported spirits, wine, beer); licensing of bonded warehouses, hotels,
       clubs, and retail shops.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

4.5  Bengal Hemp Drug Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for cultivation, collection, manufacture, possession,
       sale, and transport of ganja and bhang; licensing; maximum possession
       limits.
     Status: Modified by subsequent narcotics legislation; historical framework.

4.6  Bengal Tari (Toddy) Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules governing tapping and sale of palm toddy; licensing
       of tappers and sellers; area restrictions.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

4.7  Bengal Excise Revenue Collection Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB (Excise Commissioner / Collector)
     Description: Rules for assessment, collection, and accounting of excise
       duties and license fees.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

4.8  Bengal Opium Smoking Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules regulating licensed opium smoking establishments.
     Status: Historical; opium control now under Narcotics Control Act.

4.9  Bengal Excise (Bonded Warehouse) Rules
     Year: 1909 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for establishment and operation of bonded warehouses
       for excisable goods; storage conditions; duty deferral.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

5. INDIAN ELECTRICITY ACT, 1910 (Act IX of 1910)
   -- ELECTRICITY RULES
================================================================================

5.1  Indian Electricity Rules, 1937
     Year: 1937 (replacing earlier rules from 1910)
     Authority: Government of India (Central Electricity Authority)
     Description: Comprehensive central rules governing generation, supply,
       distribution, and use of electrical energy. Covers safety standards,
       wiring regulations, installations, meters, earthing, overhead lines,
       substations, consumer protection, electrical accidents, and inspections.
       Applied to Bengal as central legislation.
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Forms the basis of
       electrical safety regulation.

5.2  Bengal Electrical Installation Rules
     Year: 1910 onwards (revised 1937)
     Authority: GoB / Chief Electrical Inspector
     Description: Provincial rules supplementing the central rules, covering
       licensing of electrical contractors and wiremen, inspection of
       installations, approval of electrical fittings, and provincial safety
       standards.
     Status: Framework continued in Bangladesh.

5.3  Electricity Supply Licensee Rules (Bengal)
     Year: 1910 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for granting, revocation, and conditions of licenses
       for electricity supply undertakings; tariff regulation; supply
       conditions; metering requirements.
     Status: Framework evolved under Bangladesh energy regulatory structure.

5.4  Indian Electricity (Meter) Rules
     Year: 1937 (under the 1910 Act)
     Authority: GoI
     Description: Rules governing testing, installation, and reading of
       electricity meters; standards for accuracy; procedures for dispute
       resolution on meter readings.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

5.5  Rules for Electrical Accidents (Investigation)
     Year: 1937
     Authority: GoI / Chief Electrical Inspector
     Description: Rules for investigation of fatal and non-fatal electrical
       accidents; reporting requirements; inquiry procedures.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

6. COOPERATIVE SOCIETIES ACT, 1912 (Act II of 1912)
   -- COOPERATIVE RULES
================================================================================

6.1  Bengal Cooperative Societies Rules, 1912
     Year: 1912 (revised substantially 1927, 1940)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Registrar of Cooperative Societies)
     Description: Comprehensive rules for registration, management, audit,
       inspection, and liquidation of cooperative societies. Covers:
       - Application for registration and by-laws
       - Membership conditions and rights
       - Management committee election and powers
       - Financial management and audit requirements
       - Annual returns and reports
       - Dispute resolution through arbitration
       - Liquidation and winding-up procedures
     Status: Framework continued. Cooperative Societies Act 1912 remained
       applicable until replaced by Cooperative Societies Ordinance 1984
       in Bangladesh.

6.2  Bengal Cooperative Societies (Election) Rules
     Year: 1912 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Registrar
     Description: Rules for election of managing committees, qualifications
       of candidates, voting procedures, and election disputes.
     Status: Superseded by later cooperative legislation.

6.3  Bengal Cooperative Societies (Audit) Rules
     Year: 1912 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Registrar
     Description: Rules for annual audit of cooperative society accounts,
       qualifications of auditors, audit standards, and rectification.
     Status: Framework continued.

6.4  Bengal Cooperative Credit Societies Rules
     Year: 1912 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Registrar
     Description: Special rules for agricultural and non-agricultural credit
       cooperative societies; loan procedures; interest rates; security.
     Status: Framework continued.

6.5  Bengal Cooperative Land Mortgage Bank Rules
     Year: c.1930 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Registrar
     Description: Rules for cooperative land mortgage banks providing long-term
       agricultural credit against land security.
     Status: Historical.

6.6  Rules under the Bengal Cooperative Societies Act, 1940 (Bengal Act XXI of 1940)
     Year: 1940 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Registrar
     Description: Revised and expanded rules under the provincial Act replacing
       the central 1912 Act for Bengal. Covered all aspects of cooperative
       administration with greater provincial control.
     Status: Framework continued until Cooperative Societies Ordinance 1984.

7. BENGAL PUBLIC DEMANDS RECOVERY ACT, 1913 (Bengal Act III of 1913)
   -- PDR RULES
================================================================================

7.1  Bengal Public Demands Recovery Rules, 1913
     Year: 1913 onwards (revised periodically)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Board of Revenue)
     Description: Comprehensive rules for recovery of public demands
       (government dues) as arrears of land revenue through the certificate
       procedure. Covers:
       - Filing of certificates by public officers
       - Service of notice on certificate debtors
       - Procedures for objection and hearing
       - Attachment and sale of movable and immovable property
       - Arrest and detention of certificate debtors
       - Distribution of sale proceeds
       - Setting aside of sales
       - Appeals and revision
       - Forms and registers
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). BPDR Act 1913 and rules
       continue as the primary mechanism for recovery of government dues.

7.2  Forms under the BPDR Act (Bengal)
     Year: 1913 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Prescribed forms for certificate proceedings including
       certificate for recovery, notice to debtor, warrant of attachment,
       sale proclamation, sale certificate, and various returns.
     Status: In force (as adapted); forms revised periodically.

7.3  Notifications specifying "Public Demands" under BPDR Act
     Year: Various from 1913 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Notifications declaring specific government dues as "public
       demands" recoverable under the certificate procedure (taxes, fees,
       loans, rents, etc.).
     Status: Continuing process; new notifications issued as needed.

8. INDIAN FACTORIES ACT, 1911 / FACTORIES ACT, 1934
   -- FACTORIES RULES
================================================================================

8.1  Bengal Factories Rules under the Factories Act, 1911 (Act XII of 1911)
     Year: 1911 onwards
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Chief Inspector of Factories)
     Description: Early factory regulation covering working hours, employment
       of women and children, health and safety, ventilation, lighting,
       fencing of machinery, and factory registration.
     Status: Superseded by rules under the 1934 Act.

8.2  Bengal Factories Rules, 1935 (under Factories Act, 1934 -- Act XXV of 1934)
     Year: 1935 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Chief Inspector of Factories, Bengal
     Description: Comprehensive rules replacing the 1911-era rules. Covers:
       - Factory registration and licensing
       - Health provisions (cleanliness, ventilation, lighting, drinking water,
         latrines, spittoons)
       - Safety provisions (fencing of machinery, work on near machinery in
         motion, hoists and lifts, pressure plant, floors/stairs/passages,
         pits/sumps/openings, excessive weights)
       - Welfare provisions (washing facilities, first aid, canteens,
         shelters, creches)
       - Working hours for adults, adolescents, and children
       - Annual leave with wages
       - Registers, records, and returns (prescribed forms)
       - Special rules for dangerous operations (listed in schedule)
       - Inspector powers and duties
     Status: Factories Act 1934 continued in Bangladesh until Labour Act 2006.
       Bengal Factories Rules formed the basis of subsequent factory regulations.

8.3  Rules for Dangerous Operations (Bengal Factories)
     Year: 1935 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Chief Inspector of Factories
     Description: Special rules for specific hazardous industrial processes
       including lead manufacturing, chromium plating, sandblasting, aerated
       water manufacture, and chemical operations.
     Status: Framework continued under Labour Act 2006.

8.4  Bengal Factories (Exemption) Orders
     Year: Various from 1911 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Orders exempting certain classes of factories or processes
       from specific provisions of the Act (seasonal factories, small
       establishments, etc.).
     Status: Historical; superseded by later exemptions.

9. BENGAL VILLAGE SELF-GOVERNMENT ACT, 1919 (Bengal Act V of 1919)
   -- PANCHAYAT / UNION BOARD RULES
================================================================================

9.1  Bengal Union Board Rules
     Year: 1919 onwards (comprehensive revision 1935)
     Authority: Government of Bengal
     Description: Rules governing establishment, powers, and functions of
       Union Boards in rural Bengal. Covers:
       - Constitution and territorial jurisdiction
       - Election of presidents and members
       - Meetings and quorum
       - Powers and duties (roads, sanitation, water supply, public health,
         primary education oversight, registration of births and deaths)
       - Budget and financial management
       - Audit and inspection
       - Dissolution and supersession
     Status: Superseded by Basic Democracies Order 1959 (Pakistan) and
       subsequently Local Government Acts in Bangladesh. Historical significance.

9.2  Bengal Union Board Election Rules
     Year: 1919 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for election of Union Board presidents and members;
       electoral rolls; qualifications; disqualifications; election procedures;
       election disputes.
     Status: Historical; superseded.

9.3  Bengal Village Court Rules
     Year: 1919 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for constitution and procedure of village courts
       (gram adalat) established under Section 66 of the Act. Covers
       jurisdiction (minor civil and criminal matters), appointment of
       judges, procedure, appeals.
     Status: Historical; village courts now established under Village Courts
       Act 2006 (Bangladesh).

9.4  Bengal Union Board Taxation Rules
     Year: 1919 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for imposition and collection of local taxes by
       Union Boards including rate on annual value of buildings, tax on
       professions and trades, and tolls.
     Status: Historical; superseded by subsequent local government taxation.

9.5  Bengal Union Board Budget and Accounts Rules
     Year: 1919 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for preparation of annual budgets, maintenance of
       accounts, and audit of Union Board finances.
     Status: Historical; superseded.

9.6  Bengal Panchayat Rules (under Bengal Self-Government Act, 1919, and
     Bengal Panchayat Act, 1920)
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules for establishment and functioning of panchayats at
       the village level, covering minor dispute resolution, community
       welfare, and local administration.
     Status: Historical; superseded by subsequent local government legislation.

10. GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT, 1919 (9 & 10 Geo. V, c. 101)
    -- ALL RULES MADE UNDER
================================================================================

10.1 Devolution Rules, 1920
     Year: 1920
     Authority: Governor-General in Council (GoI)
     Description: Rules defining the division of subjects between the Central
       and Provincial Governments under the dyarchy system. Specified
       "reserved" and "transferred" subjects for Bengal and other provinces.
     Status: Historical; superseded by Government of India Act 1935.

10.2 Scheduled Taxes Rules
     Year: 1920
     Authority: GoI
     Description: Rules specifying taxation powers of provincial legislatures
       under the dyarchy scheme.
     Status: Historical; superseded.

10.3 Bengal Legislative Council Rules
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: Governor of Bengal / GoI
     Description: Rules of procedure and conduct of business in the Bengal
       Legislative Council established under the 1919 Act. Covers questions,
       motions, legislative procedure, committee formation, and debates.
     Status: Historical; superseded by 1935 Act rules.

10.4 Rules for Election to the Bengal Legislative Council
     Year: 1920
     Authority: GoI
     Description: Electoral rules for the Bengal Legislative Council including
       franchise qualifications, constituencies, voter registration, nomination
       and election procedures, election disputes.
     Status: Historical; superseded.

10.5 Indian Civil Service (Temporary) Rules
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: Secretary of State for India / GoI
     Description: Rules governing the Indian Civil Service during the transition
       under dyarchy, including recruitment, examination, training, and service
       conditions applicable to Bengal cadre officers.
     Status: Historical; superseded.

10.6 Central Services Rules (applicable to Bengal)
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: GoI
     Description: Rules governing central government services deployed in
       Bengal, including All-India Services (ICS, IPS) and Central Services.
     Status: Historical; framework adapted post-independence.

10.7 Bengal Government Servants' Conduct Rules (1919 Act basis)
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules governing conduct of provincial government servants
       in Bengal under the reformed constitution, covering political activities,
       private employment, acceptance of gifts, and discipline.
     Status: Framework continued through adaptation.

10.8 Bengal Financial Rules (under 1919 Act)
     Year: 1921 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Finance Department
     Description: Provincial financial rules governing expenditure, audit,
       budget, and treasury management in Bengal under dyarchy.
       Includes Bengal Treasury Rules.
     Status: Framework substantially adapted and continued.

10.9 Rules under the Indian Civil Services (Temporary Provisions) Act, 1920
     Year: 1920 onwards
     Authority: Secretary of State / GoI
     Description: Transitional service rules for ICS officers during the
       reform period.
     Status: Historical.

11. INDIAN BOILERS ACT, 1923 (Act V of 1923)
    -- BOILER RULES
================================================================================

11.1 Indian Boiler Regulations, 1923
     Year: 1923 onwards (revised 1928, 1937, 1945)
     Authority: Government of India (Central Boilers Board)
     Description: Central regulations prescribing standards for boiler
       construction, materials, design, fittings, and safety appurtenances.
       Applicable uniformly across British India including Bengal.
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Indian Boilers Act 1923
       continues as the primary boiler safety legislation.

11.2 Bengal Boiler Rules
     Year: 1923 onwards
     Authority: Government of Bengal / Chief Inspector of Boilers, Bengal
     Description: Provincial rules supplementing the central regulations.
       Covers:
       - Registration of boilers in Bengal
       - Inspection procedures and periodicity
       - Fees for registration and inspection
       - Certification of boiler attendants and engine drivers
       - Reporting of boiler accidents
       - Exemptions for specific classes of boilers
     Status: In force in Bangladesh (as adapted).

11.3 Rules for Examination and Certification of Boiler Attendants (Bengal)
     Year: 1923 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Chief Inspector of Boilers
     Description: Rules for qualification examinations for boiler attendants
       (first class, second class), engine drivers, and boiler operation
       supervisors. Covers syllabus, examination centers, and certificate
       validity.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

11.4 Rules for Boiler Accident Investigation (Bengal)
     Year: 1923 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Chief Inspector of Boilers
     Description: Rules for reporting, investigation, and inquiry into
       boiler explosions and accidents; composition of inquiry committees.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

12. WORKMEN'S COMPENSATION ACT, 1923 (Act VIII of 1923)
    -- WC RULES
================================================================================

12.1 Bengal Workmen's Compensation Rules, 1924
     Year: 1924 (revised 1933, 1939, 1946)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Labour Department)
     Description: Comprehensive rules for implementation of the WC Act in
       Bengal. Covers:
       - Filing of claims before the Commissioner for Workmen's Compensation
       - Calculation and assessment of compensation for death, permanent
         total disability, permanent partial disability, and temporary
         disability
       - Procedure before the Commissioner
       - Deposit of compensation amounts
       - Distribution of compensation in death cases
       - Medical examination and certification
       - Review of compensation orders
       - Registration of agreements
       - Notices and reports of accidents by employers
       - Prescribed forms (Form A through Form T)
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Workmen's Compensation Act
       1923 continued until consolidated into Labour Act 2006.

12.2 Schedule of Injuries (Bengal notifications)
     Year: Various notifications
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Notifications specifying percentage of loss of earning
       capacity for various types of injuries listed in Schedule I of the Act.
     Status: Framework continued.

12.3 Rules for Notice of Accidents (Bengal)
     Year: 1924 onwards
     Authority: GoB
     Description: Rules requiring employers to give notice of accidents
       causing death or bodily injury to the Commissioner and specified
       authorities within prescribed time limits.
     Status: Framework continued.

15. INDIAN FOREST ACT, 1927 (Act XVI of 1927)
    -- FOREST RULES, PRIVATE FOREST RULES
================================================================================

15.1 Bengal Forest Rules (under Indian Forest Act, 1927)
     Year: 1927 onwards (comprehensively revised 1935, 1945)
     Authority: Government of Bengal (Forest Department)
     Description: Comprehensive rules for forest management in Bengal. Covers:
       - Declaration and management of reserved forests
       - Rights of communities in reserved forests
       - Protected forests regulations
       - Transit rules for timber and forest produce
       - Grazing and pasture rules
       - Collection of non-timber forest produce (NTFPs)
       - Penalties for forest offences
       - Forest settlement procedures
       - Compounding of offences
     Status: IN FORCE in Bangladesh (as adapted). Indian Forest Act 1927
       remains the primary forest legislation in Bangladesh.

15.2 Bengal Forest Transit Rules
     Year: 1927 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Conservator of Forests
     Description: Rules governing transit of timber and other forest produce
       through Bengal territory. Covers transit passes, check posts, marking
       of timber, seizure of unmarked produce, and penalties.
     Status: In force (as adapted).

15.3 Rules for Disposal of Forest Produce (Bengal)
     Year: 1927 onwards
     Authority: GoB / Conservator of Forests
     Description: Rules for sale and disposal of forest produce from reserved

ADDITIONAL SUBORDINATE LEGISLATION NOTES
================================================================================

The following additional subordinate instruments, though not listed as separate
items under the 28 parent Acts above, are cross-referenced and relevant:

A.  HIGH COURT RULES relating to multiple parent Acts:
    The High Court of Calcutta issued comprehensive rules and circular orders
    that applied across multiple parent statutes. The Appellate Side Rules,
    Original Side Rules, and Practice Directions constituted a major body of
    subordinate legislation governing court procedure.

B.  GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT 1935 -- ADAPTATION ORDERS:
    The India (Provisional Constitution) Order, 1947 and subsequent adaptation
    orders under the Indian Independence Act, 1947 formally continued these
    subordinate legislations in the successor states. For East Bengal/East
    Pakistan, the "Adaptation of Laws Order, 1947" and "Adaptation of Central
    Acts and Ordinances Order, 1949" were the key instruments.

C.  BENGAL GAZETTE NOTIFICATIONS:
    Thousands of individual notifications were published in the Bengal Gazette
    during 1900-1947, many of which constitute subordinate legislation
    (declaring applicability of Acts to specific areas, specifying exemptions,
    fixing rates, appointing authorities, etc.). A complete enumeration would
    require systematic review of all Bengal Gazette volumes for this period.
